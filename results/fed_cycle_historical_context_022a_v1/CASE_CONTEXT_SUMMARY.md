## B02 — 1987-1989 tightening/easing sequence

- **RETROSPECTIVE_EVENT_CONTEXT** (1987-10-19; AFTER_T03_BEFORE_T04_FIRST_CUT): Federal Reserve History records Black Monday on 1987-10-19, when the DJIA fell 22.6% in one session amid a global market crash. [known/observable at the time]
  - Use: Use as dated market-stress context between B02 sub-cycles.
  - Guardrail: The source is retrospective; the crash itself was contemporaneously observable. Do not claim the crash was caused by the Fed path or that later B02 cuts were mechanically determined by this event.
- **RETROSPECTIVE_EVENT_CONTEXT** (1987-10-20; AFTER_T03_BEFORE_T04_FIRST_CUT): Federal Reserve History records the Fed's public readiness to provide liquidity after Black Monday and describes operational efforts to support market functioning. [known/observable at the time]
  - Use: Use to explain the financial-stability response surrounding the 1987 sub-cycle.
  - Guardrail: The historical essay was written later, although the liquidity statement was public on 1987-10-20. Do not equate liquidity support with a validated asset-bottom signal.
- **RETROSPECTIVE_DATING** (1987-1989; EPISODE_BACKGROUND): Federal Reserve History notes that the 1987 stock-market crash was not followed by an economic recession; NBER chronology places the next U.S. business-cycle peak in July 1990. [not contemporaneously available as this later classification/context]
  - Use: Use to distinguish severe market stress from an automatically coincident recession.
  - Guardrail: Recession classification is retrospective and must not be presented as a real-time signal available during B02. Do not generalize that a crash never leads to recession or that B02 predicts future cycles.

## B03 — 1994-1995 tightening/easing cycle

- **CONTEMPORANEOUS_POLICY_CONTEXT** (1995-07-06; AT_FIRST_CUT): The July 1995 FOMC statement said inflationary pressures had receded after the tightening initiated in early 1994 and announced a modest easing; the federal funds rate declined 25 bp from about 6% to about 5.75%. [known/observable at the time]
  - Use: Use as the Fed's stated rationale for the first cut in the 1994-95 case.
  - Guardrail: The transcript itself was released later, but the quoted policy statement was released at the event. Do not convert the statement into proof that the subsequent asset path was caused by the cut.
- **RETROSPECTIVE_DATING** (1994-1995; EPISODE_BACKGROUND): NBER's current chronology places the U.S. expansion between the March 1991 trough and March 2001 peak, so the 1994-95 case is not dated as an NBER recession episode. [not contemporaneously available as this later classification/context]
  - Use: Use to contrast B03 with recession-associated later cases.
  - Guardrail: Business-cycle dating is retrospective and the absence of an NBER recession label is not a contemporaneous forecast. Do not label the episode a guaranteed 'soft landing' template for other cycles.
- **RETROSPECTIVE_EVENT_CONTEXT** (1994-1995; EPISODE_BACKGROUND): The Federal Reserve's historical materials page notes that the FOMC first announced meeting outcomes in February 1994 and in February 1995 formalized immediate public communication of all policy-stance changes. [not contemporaneously available as this later classification/context]
  - Use: Use to explain why 1994-95 is a transition-era communication case.
  - Guardrail: This is retrospective institutional context about the communication regime. Do not compare statement wording across eras without accounting for communication-regime changes.

## B04 — 1999-2001 tightening/easing cycle

- **CONTEMPORANEOUS_POLICY_CONTEXT** (2001-01-03; AT_FIRST_CUT): Federal Reserve records describe the January 3, 2001 intermeeting 50 bp cut as occurring amid weakening sales and production, lower consumer confidence, tight conditions in parts of financial markets, and high energy prices weighing on purchasing power. [known/observable at the time]
  - Use: Use as contemporaneous policy/economic context for the 2001 first cut.
  - Guardrail: The report was published shortly after the action and summarizes contemporaneous conditions; later recession dating was not yet known. Do not say the January cut was taken because an NBER recession had already been officially identified.
- **RETROSPECTIVE_DATING** (2001-03_to_2001-11; POST_FIRST_CUT): NBER later dated the 2001 recession from the March 2001 peak to the November 2001 trough; the March peak was announced on 2001-11-26. [not contemporaneously available as this later classification/context]
  - Use: Use to show the difference between first-cut timing and later official recession dating.
  - Guardrail: At the January 3 first cut, the later March recession peak and its NBER classification were not known. Do not back-project the later NBER label into January 2001 information.
- **POST_ANCHOR_SHOCK** (2001-09-11; POST_FIRST_CUT): Federal Reserve History describes the September 11 attacks as a major shock that created massive dislocations in U.S. financial markets and prompted a broad Federal Reserve liquidity response. [not contemporaneously available as this later classification/context]
  - Use: Use to explain why the 12-month post-cut path contains a large later exogenous shock.
  - Guardrail: September 11 occurred more than eight months after the January first cut and cannot be part of the cut's original rationale. Do not imply the January 2001 cut anticipated September 11.

## B05 — 2004-2007 tightening/easing cycle

- **CONTEMPORANEOUS_POLICY_CONTEXT** (2007-09-18; AT_FIRST_CUT): The September 18, 2007 FOMC statement cut the federal funds target 50 bp to 4.75% and said tighter credit conditions could intensify the housing correction and restrain growth more generally. [known/observable at the time]
  - Use: Use as the Fed's stated risk context at the 2007 first cut.
  - Guardrail: This is contemporaneous stated policy context, not a later causal decomposition. Do not say the Fed already knew the full scale of the later financial crisis.
- **RETROSPECTIVE_EVENT_CONTEXT** (2007; PRE_AND_AROUND_FIRST_CUT): Federal Reserve History documents that subprime-mortgage stress was already visible in 2007, including New Century's April bankruptcy, mortgage-security downgrades, lender closures and tightening mortgage credit. [known/observable at the time]
  - Use: Use to establish that housing/credit stress predated the September first cut.
  - Guardrail: The synthesis is retrospective; individual stress events were observable at the time but their eventual systemic scale was not fully known. Do not claim all subsequent crisis outcomes were foreseeable in September 2007.
- **RETROSPECTIVE_DATING** (2007-12_to_2009-06; POST_FIRST_CUT): NBER later dated the Great Recession from a December 2007 peak to a June 2009 trough; the December 2007 peak was announced only on 2008-12-01. [not contemporaneously available as this later classification/context]
  - Use: Use to separate contemporaneous credit stress from later recession classification.
  - Guardrail: The recession start was retrospectively dated after the September 2007 cut; the official peak determination came about a year later. Do not state that an officially identified recession already existed at the September 2007 decision date.

## B06 — 2015-2019 tightening/easing cycle

- **CONTEMPORANEOUS_POLICY_CONTEXT** (2019-07-31; AT_FIRST_CUT): The July 31, 2019 FOMC statement said the labor market remained strong and activity was rising moderately, while global developments and muted inflation pressures motivated a 25 bp reduction to a 2.00-2.25% target range. [known/observable at the time]
  - Use: Use as the first-cut rationale for the 2019 case.
  - Guardrail: This records the Committee's stated contemporaneous rationale and should not be rewritten using later pandemic information. Do not say the July 2019 cut was a response to COVID-19.
- **RETROSPECTIVE_DATING** (2020-02_to_2020-04; POST_FIRST_CUT): NBER determined that the expansion peaked in February 2020 and that the recession trough occurred in April 2020; the peak determination was announced on 2020-06-08. [not contemporaneously available as this later classification/context]
  - Use: Use to distinguish the 2019 easing cycle from the later 2020 recession.
  - Guardrail: The recession began months after the July 2019 first cut and was dated retrospectively. Do not treat the February 2020 recession date as information available in July 2019.
- **POST_ANCHOR_SHOCK** (2020-03; POST_FIRST_CUT): In March 2020 the Federal Reserve explicitly said the coronavirus outbreak had disrupted economic activity and financial conditions and posed sharply increased downside risks, leading to emergency easing to the effective lower bound. [not contemporaneously available as this later classification/context]
  - Use: Use to explain why the 12-month post-July-2019 market path includes a qualitatively new shock.
  - Guardrail: COVID-19 was a later shock and must not be inserted into the July 2019 first-cut rationale. Do not imply the 2019 cut anticipated the pandemic.

## B07 — 2022-2024 tightening/easing cycle

- **CONTEMPORANEOUS_POLICY_CONTEXT** (2024-09-18; AT_FIRST_CUT): The September 18, 2024 FOMC statement said activity remained solid, job gains had slowed, unemployment had risen but remained low, inflation had made further progress, and risks to employment and inflation were judged roughly balanced; the target range was lowered 50 bp. [known/observable at the time]
  - Use: Use as the 2024 first-cut policy context.
  - Guardrail: This is the Committee's stated contemporaneous rationale, not a guarantee of a soft landing or future asset returns. Do not label the decision as proof that recession risk had disappeared.
- **RETROSPECTIVE_DATING** (2026-09-25; CURRENT_SOURCE_AUDIT): On the NBER Business Cycle Dating page audited on 2026-09-25, the most recent recognized U.S. business-cycle peak remains February 2020. [not contemporaneously available as this later classification/context]
  - Use: Use only to report the current official chronology status as of the audit date.
  - Guardrail: NBER dating is deliberately retrospective and absence of a newer dated peak is not a real-time recession forecast. Do not convert absence of a new NBER peak into a claim that recession is impossible or cannot later be dated.
- **CONTEMPORANEOUS_POLICY_CONTEXT** (2024-09-18; AT_FIRST_CUT): September 2024 FOMC minutes report that most participants saw downside risks to employment as having increased while upside inflation risks had diminished, contributing to a roughly balanced dual-mandate risk assessment. [known/observable at the time]
  - Use: Use to explain the risk-management framing behind the 2024 cut.
  - Guardrail: Minutes provide contemporaneous-meeting reasoning but were released after the meeting. Do not portray a balanced-risk assessment as an asset-market timing signal.
